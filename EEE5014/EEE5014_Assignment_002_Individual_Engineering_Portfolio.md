## Aim

Create an individual, evidence-led professional portfolio from the laboratory pump condition-monitoring and protective-interlock project. The portfolio must demonstrate how you contributed as an engineer and team member, how you developed technical capability, and how you will use reflection and CPD to guide future learning.

## Learning Outcomes

- **LO2:** Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader of a team. (C16)
- **LO5:** Plan and record self-learning and development as the foundation for lifelong learning/CPD. (C18)

## Vocational Context

The portfolio relates to the group project completed for Assessment 001. The operational prototype is a shared outcome, but this assessment is individual. You may cite shared evidence, but you must identify your own work and give personal analysis rather than reproduce group material.

Where relevant, identify the project platform used (Arduino Uno R3, ESP32-S1, ESP32-C3, or tutor-approved equivalent) and explain how its capabilities or limitations affected your personal technical contribution and learning.

## Tasks

### Task 2.1: Individual Contribution and Team Effectiveness

Create a documented account of how you functioned individually and within the project team. The evidence should make it possible for a reviewer to distinguish your contribution from the shared group outcome. Include:

- Your agreed role(s), responsibilities, key interfaces/dependencies with other team members, and any change in role during the project.
- Dated activity log showing planned task, actual work undertaken, outcome, evidence link, time/effort estimate, blocker, decision, feedback, and next action.
- At least three substantial personal technical contributions. For each one, state the problem/requirement, your approach, artefact produced, evidence of test or review, result, and what you would improve. Suitable contributions include requirements work, circuit design, component investigation, firmware module, test design, integration/debugging, user-interface design, documentation, or demonstration delivery.
- Evidence for every contribution, for example annotated commits, design versions, photographs, test records, code review, meeting minutes, issue-tracker extracts, or tutor observation. Briefly explain what the evidence proves.
- Critical reflection on team planning, task distribution, communication, dependencies, technical disagreement, quality review, and the effect of any missed milestone.
- Reflection on leadership/followership, feedback, peer support, and equitable/inclusive participation. Give one specific example rather than a general statement.

### Task 2.2: Technical Learning and CPD

Evaluate your learning and plan future development. Use evidence from the project rather than only describing what you enjoyed or found difficult. Include:

- Self-assessment against at least four embedded-systems technical or professional skills, using a before-project and after-project confidence/competence rating with a short evidence-based explanation of the change.
- Learning evidence from areas such as requirements engineering, state-machine design, C/C++ programming, hardware prototyping, communications, testing, debugging, documentation, sustainability, or user-centred design. Connect each selected skill to a specific project artefact or event.
- Critical case study of one significant technical challenge: initial hypothesis, investigation steps, data/logs or other evidence considered, alternatives rejected, resolution, retest, and lesson learned. Suitable topics include sensor noise, timing conflict, board-voltage incompatibility, I2C/UART communication failure, state-machine error, or integration issue.
- CPD plan with at least three SMART actions. For each action provide a target date, learning activity/resource, success measure, intended evidence, and an explanation of why the action is relevant to future embedded-systems work.
- Reflection on how records, feedback, and self-directed learning support future UK-SPEC competence and employability.
