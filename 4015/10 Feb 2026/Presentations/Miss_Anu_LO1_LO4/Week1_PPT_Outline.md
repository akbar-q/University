## Slide 4 — Why PLCs (not general PCs) in industry?
**On-slide:**
- Robust I/O and electrical noise tolerance
- Deterministic scan cycle
- Maintainable logic (IEC 61131-3)
- Lifecycle + documentation + fault finding

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
- Tie to plant realities: downtime, maintenance engineers, safety constraints.

---

- Communications interface
- Programming device

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**

## Slide 6 — PLC configurations (P1 core content)
**On-slide:**
- **Unitary/compact:** fixed I/O, small footprint
- **Modular:** add/remove I/O, flexible
- **Rack mounted:** larger systems, many modules, high expandability

**Speaker notes (10 min):**
- Explain “configuration” as physical + scalable architecture.
- Give one real advantage + one limitation per type.

---

## Slide 7 — Selection factors (how engineers choose)
**On-slide:**
- I/O count and type
- Scan time/performance needs
- Environment (noise, vibration, temperature)
- Expandability
- Cost + maintenance

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Keep it Level 4: avoid deep hardware design; focus on sensible selection.

---

## Slide 8 — Typical applications by configuration
**On-slide:**
- Compact: small machines, simple sequences
- Modular: cells with varied sensors/actuators
- Rack: larger lines, many zones, more comms

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Provide 2–3 examples each: packaging machine, pump station, assembly line.

## Slide 16 — Common mistakes (and how to fix)
**On-slide:**
- Mistake: “Modular is better” (too generic)
- Fix: specify *why* in a context
- Mistake: no limitations
- Fix: always include constraints

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
