# EEE6011 Coursework Format Reference

Source: `EEE6011_CW_2025-2026.docx`

## Assessment Details

- Programme: BEng (Hons) Electrical & Electronic Engineering (Advanced Standing)
- Level: HE6
- Module: EEE6011, Electrical Machines and Power Electronic Drives
- Assessment: Coursework 1 of 1, *Performance Analysis of DC and AC Machines*
- Weighting: 40% of the module
- Brief submission date: 25 April 2026
- Required length: 2,000 words or equivalent report

## Academic Integrity and Tool Use

The brief classifies this assessment as **Category A**: no Generative AI tools are permitted. Grammar and spell checkers may be used only to correct individual words and sentences. Presenting unacknowledged AI-generated content as original work may be treated as academic misconduct.

The brief also requires a declaration at the end of the assessment identifying software tools, including any Generative AI applications, used during its development and completion.

For this coursework, assistance should therefore be limited to tutor-approved support and general study guidance that does not create or develop assessed content.

## Required Report Format

1. Title page stating the programme name, module title, assessment title, student number, marking tutor, and submission date.
2. Contents page with page numbers.
3. Consolidated technical report with a distinct section for each of the six tasks.
4. Each task section must contain an introduction, methodology, findings, and conclusion.
5. Labelled graphs, tables, calculations, testing evidence, comparisons, observations, and discussion within the relevant task section.
6. MATLAB code that is well-commented and properly formatted.
7. Detailed design elements, layouts, and specifications where required.
8. References using the University of Bolton/Greater Manchester Harvard referencing style.
9. Appendices containing experimental setup photos, the original technical diary, and plagiarism coversheet. Include supplementary calculations and code where appropriate.
10. A word count at the end, excluding references, figures, tables, and appendices.
11. Software/GAI tools declaration at the end, subject to the Category A restriction above.

## Presentation Requirements

- Word-process in Arial or Calibri Light, 12 pt.
- Use double line spacing.
- Number every page.
- Write carefully with accurate spelling and punctuation.
- Demonstrate analytical competence through calculations and reasoned engineering arguments.
- Use a clear technical-report style: report evidence, interpret it, compare it against theory/experiment, and support conclusions with references.

### Task 1.1: DC Motor Performance Analysis

- Model DC series and DC shunt motors in MATLAB/Simulink.
- Produce Speed-Torque ($N-T_a$), Speed-Armature Current ($N-I_a$), and Torque-Armature Current ($T_a-I_a$) curves for both motors.
- Analyse the effect of load torque on speed and current.
- Evaluate suitability for lifting equipment and conveyor systems.
- Address maintenance requirements, reliability, downtime, maintenance planning, and operational continuity.

### Task 1.2: Validation and Implementation Management

- Validate simulations using laboratory experimental data.
- Compare simulated and measured speed-torque, speed-current, and torque-current characteristics.
- Explain differences using non-ideal effects, including mechanical losses, temperature rise, magnetic saturation, and brush voltage drop.
- Evaluate technical, operational, and equipment-integration risks.
- Propose validation/testing, commissioning quality assurance, transition planning, and operator/maintenance training strategies.

### Task 1.3: DC Chopper Drive and Commercial Evaluation

- Model Class A and Class B DC chopper drives in MATLAB/Simulink.
- Implement PWM armature-voltage/current control.
- Investigate duty-cycle effects on speed, armature current and voltage, and electromagnetic torque.
- Analyse voltage, current, speed, and torque waveforms.
- Evaluate lifecycle costs, energy use, operating costs, maintenance/downtime, industrial electrical-safety compliance, intellectual property, and MATLAB/Simulink licensing.

### Task 2.1: Induction Motor Performance

- Simulate a three-phase induction motor.
- Plot and analyse the speed-torque characteristic.
- Assess changing load-torque effects, operating performance, and stable versus unstable operating regions.
- Record rotor speed, electromagnetic torque, power factor, mechanical output power, and efficiency.
- Discuss suitability for industrial conveyors and material handling.

### Task 2.2: VFD-Controlled Induction Motor

- Develop a VFD-controlled induction-motor model with V/f speed control.
- Simulate different load and frequency conditions.
- Analyse speed response, torque, stator current, power factor, output power, and efficiency.
- Critically assess energy efficiency, reduced emissions, process control, cost savings, and environmental/societal impact.

### Task 2.3: PWM PMSM Drive

- Develop a three-phase voltage-source inverter driving a PMSM.
- Implement sinusoidal PWM (SPWM) switching.
- Integrate and simulate the PMSM drive.
- Present speed, electromagnetic torque, stator-current, and rotor-position results.
- Critically assess efficiency, environmental benefits, societal impacts, and industrial suitability.

## Research and Referencing Standard

- Expected reference-list size at HE6: 15 to 20 sources.
- Minimum: three refereed academic journals and five academic books.
- Prefer current, authoritative engineering literature alongside the module's recommended texts.
- Cite sources where technical claims, equations, design choices, standards, commercial figures, legal matters, environmental claims, or comparisons depend on external evidence.
- Use consistent Harvard in-text citations and a complete reference list.

## Grade Standard

For a first-class mark (70%+), the report needs rigorous and creative engineering solutions; accurate and critical simulation/performance analysis; consideration of operation, maintenance, lifecycle implications, and system optimisation; well-supported commercial, economic, environmental, and societal evaluation; broad current research; and excellent academic style and referencing.

## Word Count and Submission Rules

- Up to 10% above 2,000 words (up to 2,200): no penalty.
- More than 10% and up to 20% over (2,201 to 2,400): 5 marks deducted, subject to the pass-mark safeguard stated in the brief.
- More than 20% over (above 2,400): capped at a pass where the work would otherwise pass.
- Submit a Word document through Moodle/Turnitin unless the module tutor states otherwise.
- If Moodle upload fails, email the electronic copy to the module tutor before the deadline.
- Up to 7 calendar days late: 10 marks deducted, subject to the pass-mark safeguard.
- More than 7 days late: non-submission, with no mark recorded.
