# Week 7 Presentation Outline — Unit 5041
## LO3 (D3) Critical evaluation vs plan + LO4 preparation (presentation build + reflective practice setup)

Project pathways: **MATLAB simulation/model-based OR hardware**.

Session length: **4 hours**.

Main outcome: students produce a strong **D3 evaluation** (critical analysis of outcomes vs original plan, with justified recommendations), and build an individual presentation draft aligned to LO4 (P7) while setting up reflection/PD evidence (P8/M6/D4).

---

## Slide 1 — Title
**On-slide:**
- Week 7: Critical evaluation (D3) + presentation build
- Turn evidence into conclusions and improvements

**Speaker notes (2 min):**
- Today is about “engineering judgement”: what worked, what didn’t, why, and what next.

---

## Slide 2 — What must be finished after Week 7
**On-slide:**
- LO3 report: near-final with evaluation + recommendations
- Presentation: first full draft + rehearsal plan
- Reflection/PD: evidence gathered and structured

**Speaker notes (4–5 min):**
- You are aiming to reduce Week 8 risk.

---

## Slide 3 — Distinction driver: what D3 actually demands
**On-slide:**
- Critically analyse outcomes against the original plan
- Make justified recommendations for improvements

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- D3 is NOT “we learned a lot” or “time was short”.
- D3 IS:
  - compare planned vs actual
  - explain variance causes
  - quantify impacts
  - propose improvements that are specific and feasible

---

## Slide 4 — D3 structure: a template students can follow
**On-slide:**
1) Baseline plan summary
2) Objective-by-objective outcomes
3) Variance analysis (scope/time/cost/risk)
4) Root cause of variances
5) Recommendations + priorities

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Explain that this is basically the “evaluation” section of the report.

---

## Slide 5 — Baseline plan summary: what to include (1 page)
**On-slide:**
- Original milestones + dates
- Original scope statement
- Key dependencies
- Key risks

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
- Evidence: Jira baseline screenshots + milestone list.

---

## Slide 6 — Objective-by-objective evaluation table (required)
**On-slide:**
- Objective / KPI
- Target threshold
- Measured result (mean, n)
- Pass/Fail
- Evidence link
- Notes/limitations

**Speaker notes (10–12 min):**
- Tell them: this single table makes marking easy.
- Push for simple statistics:
  - mean + min/max + n (number of trials)

---

## Slide 7 — Writing “limitations” properly (not excuses)
**On-slide:**
- State limitation
- Explain cause
- Explain impact on results
- Propose mitigation or future work

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Example:
  - “Flow sensor not available; pump flow simulated. Impact: cannot validate real flow stability. Future: add flow sensor, re-run tests Txx.”

---

## Slide 8 — Variance analysis: scope/time/cost/risk
**On-slide:**
- Scope variance: what changed?
- Schedule variance: what slipped?
- Cost/time variance: planned vs actual
- Risk variance: what new risks appeared?

**Speaker notes (10–12 min):**
- Require at least 3 variances with root causes.
- Tie to Jira Change and Risk issues.

---

## Slide 9 — Root-cause of project variances (common in student embedded projects)
**On-slide:**
- Integration delay (interfaces not frozen)
- Hardware delivery/wiring issues
- Toolchain/library mismatch
- Underestimated testing effort

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Have them pick the biggest one and explain:
  - what signal should have warned earlier?
  - what mitigation would prevent it next time?

---

## Slide 10 — Recommendation writing (D3): quality bar
**On-slide:**
- Must be specific
- Must be justified by evidence
- Must have priority and rationale

**Speaker notes (10–12 min):**
- Good recommendation pattern:
  - “Implement non-blocking scheduling for sensor reads and telemetry to reduce missed events. Evidence: missed CMD occurrences reduced from 2/10 → 0/10 after change.”
- Bad recommendation:
  - “Improve code.”

---

## Slide 11 — Prioritisation: impact/effort matrix
**On-slide:**
- High impact / low effort first
- Include safety/compliance actions early

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
- Helps show professional judgement.

---

## Slide 12 — Individual project evaluation (required prompts)
**On-slide:**
- Which dependency caused the most impact?
- Which assumption/interface change caused rework?
- How did Jira help (or not)?
- What would you baseline/freeze earlier next time?

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- This gives excellent material for both D3 (project) and D4/M6 (reflection).

---

## Slide 13 — LO4 (P7) presentation: what “technically literate audience” means
**On-slide:**
- Assume audience understands engineering terms
- Don’t assume they know your project
- Show evidence, not marketing

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
- Explain: your assessor is technically literate.

---

## Slide 14 — Presentation structure (8–12 slides recommended)
**On-slide:**
1) Problem + aim
2) Requirements + success criteria
3) Architecture/model overview
4) Method (plan + tests)
5) Results (KPIs)
6) Fault handling demo
7) Evaluation vs plan
8) Recommendations

**Speaker notes (10–12 min):**
- Emphasise:
  - show 1 integration test
  - show 1 fault response
  - show 1 KPI table

---

## Slide 15 — How to avoid “busy slides” (and still be technical)
**On-slide:**
- One message per slide
- Use diagrams and tables
- Put detail in speaker notes

**Speaker notes (6–8 min):**
- Give a rule:
  - 6–8 bullets max, large font.

---

## Slide 16 — Feedback session planning (P7)
**On-slide:**
- Who is invited?
- What questions will you ask?
- How will you record feedback?

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**
- Provide suggested feedback questions:
  - “Was the fault handling clear?”
  - “Was the UI understandable?”
  - “What’s the first improvement you’d request?”

---

## Slide 17 — Reflective practice (P8/M6/D4): you must use a model
**On-slide:**

  - Kolb

**Speaker notes (8–10 min):**

## Slide 18 — Reflection quality bar (D4)
**On-slide:**
- Critical evaluation of end-to-end delivery
- Evidence-based (not feelings only)
- Addresses third-party feedback
- Specific action plan with timelines

**Speaker notes (10–12 min):**
- Example action:
