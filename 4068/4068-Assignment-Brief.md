## Vocational scenario or context
Automated Solutions Inc. (ASI), a manufacturer of consumer electronics, is modernising its final packaging line. The company wants to automate the pick-and-place stage where finished electronic devices are taken from a moving conveyor and placed into labelled boxes.

You are working as a Robotics Systems Engineer for Precision Automation Technologies (PAT). You must review suitable industrial robot systems, explain how a safe robot cell should be designed, program and test a robot solution for the automated task, and investigate the wider economic impact of robotics and smart factory integration. Your work must balance throughput, repeatability, safety, maintainability, and commercial viability.

## Task 1: Industrial Robot Selection, Application Review, and Safety-Compliant Justification - Technical Report

### What you must produce
A technical report that reviews industrial robot types and applications, describes selection criteria, analyses the features and operation of a six-axis robot, and evaluates the selection of a safety-compliant robot system for the ASI pick-and-place application.

### Your report must address

1. **Manufacturing application review**
- Review the ASI packaging process and the role of robotics within it.
- Explain what is being handled, including product size, mass, fragility, orientation, and throughput expectations.
- Identify key operational requirements such as cycle time, repeatability, workspace, payload, and integration with the conveyor.

2. **Industrial robot types and applications**
- Review relevant robot types used in manufacturing, for example Cartesian, SCARA, delta, collaborative, and articulated six-axis robots.
- Compare how each robot type is applied in manufacturing industries such as electronics, automotive, food, and packaging.
- Identify which robot architectures are most relevant to high-speed pick-and-place applications.

3. **Selection criteria**
- Describe the selection criteria for industrial robot applications, including:
  - payload
  - reach and working envelope
  - repeatability and accuracy
  - speed and acceleration
  - degrees of freedom
  - end-effector compatibility
  - installation footprint
  - control and communication compatibility
  - maintainability and cost

4. **Six-axis robot analysis**
- Analyse the features and operation of a six-axis industrial robot within the ASI manufacturing application.
- Explain joint movement, flexibility of motion, orientation control, and limitations compared with simpler robot types.
- Support your analysis with a labelled diagram or manufacturer data where appropriate.

5. **Recommended robot system**
- Recommend a suitable industrial robot system for the given manufacturing application.
- Justify your recommendation using technical specifications, operating requirements, and application fit.
- Include a simple system block diagram showing robot, controller, sensors, conveyor, PLC or other interfacing devices.

6. **Safety-compliant selection evaluation**
- Evaluate the selection of a safety-compliant industrial robot system for the given manufacturing application.
- Consider robot-integrated safety functions, controller safety features, compatibility with guarding and interlocks, and suitability for safe use within the ASI packaging cell.
- Make a reasoned judgement on why the selected system is appropriate for the application from both an operational and safety-compliance perspective.

### Checklist of evidence required
- Manufacturing application review
- Comparison of industrial robot types and applications
- Selection criteria discussion
- Six-axis robot analysis
- Recommended robot system with justification
- Safety-compliant selection evaluation
- System block diagram
- Harvard references

## Task 2: Robot Cell Safety and Functional Safety Analysis - Safety Report

### What you must produce
A safety report that outlines functional safety analysis methods, explains robot cell safety criteria, and develops a hazard and risk assessment for the ASI robot cell.

### Your report must address

1. **Functional safety analysis**
- Outline the principles and methods of functional safety analysis within automated manufacturing.
- Explain how methods such as hazard identification, risk estimation, risk reduction, performance level or safety integrity considerations, and validation are used in robot applications.
- Refer to relevant standards such as ISO 10218, ISO 13849, ANSI/RIA R15.06, IEC 61508, or equivalent guidance used by your centre.

2. **Robot cell safety criteria**
- Explain the safety criteria for robot cells within manufacturing applications.
- Include safeguarding measures such as fixed guarding, interlocked gates, light curtains, area scanners, emergency stops, safe speed, safe stop, lockout-tagout, warning signage, and safe maintenance access.
- Explain how electrical, mechanical, pneumatic, and human-factor risks are managed.

3. **Hazard and risk assessment**
- Develop a hazard and risk assessment for the industrial robot manufacturing system at ASI.
- Include hazards such as collision, entrapment, dropped product, end-effector failure, electrical shock, unexpected restart, conveyor interaction, and maintenance access.
- Present your findings in a structured risk table that identifies hazard, cause, consequence, initial risk, control measures, and residual risk.

### Checklist of evidence required
- Functional safety analysis overview
- Robot cell safety criteria explanation
- Completed hazard and risk assessment
- Relevant standards and legislation references
- Harvard references

## Task 3: Industrial Robot Programming and Validation - Programming Portfolio and Professional Discussion

### What you must produce
A programming portfolio supported by simulation or practical evidence, together with professional discussion notes, that investigates robot programming languages and methods, programs a robot to perform a task, analyses offline and online programming approaches, and shows the design, development, and testing of a robot program for a series of automated industrial tasks.

### Your submission must address

1. **Programming languages and methods**
- Investigate the range of programming languages and methods available for industrial robots.
- Include relevant approaches such as joint-level, robot-level, and high-level programming.
- Refer to suitable tools and technologies such as teach pendants, jog controls, simulation environments, PLC integration, MATLAB, Python, RobotStudio, RoboGuide, MotoSim, WinCaps, ABB RAPID, KUKA KRL, or Yaskawa INFORM where appropriate.

2. **Simple task programming**
- Program an industrial robot to perform a simple pick-and-place task for the ASI packaging line.
- Show sequence logic for pick, transfer, place, and return.
- Include end-effector control, sensor or interlock use, and basic fault handling.

3. **Offline and online programming analysis**
- Analyse offline and online programming methods for industrial robots.
- Compare simulation-based programming with teach pendant or live cell programming.
- Discuss strengths and limitations in terms of safety, commissioning time, accuracy, flexibility, downtime, and debugging.

4. **Design, development, and testing of automated tasks**
- Design, develop, and test a robot program for a series of automated industrial tasks.
- Extend the simple task to include variations such as different box positions, conveyor timing changes, object orientation changes, or sensor-triggered conditions.
- Record evidence of testing, modifications, and final performance.

5. **Professional discussion evidence**
- Prepare discussion notes that explain your programming decisions, testing process, and lessons learned.
- Be ready to explain command structure, motion control, interlocks, and how faults were identified and corrected.

### Checklist of evidence required
- Review of programming languages and methods
- Robot program or pseudocode
- Flowchart, sequence chart, or screenshots of program structure
- Offline or simulation evidence and, where available, live test evidence
- Comparison of offline and online programming methods
- Test results for a series of automated tasks
- Professional discussion notes
- Harvard references

## Task 4: Smart Factory Integration and Global Economic Impact - Presentation and Evaluative Report

### What you must produce
A presentation with speaker notes, or a presentation supported by a short evaluative report, that assesses collaborative robots, investigates advances in industrial robot technology, analyses the contribution of artificial intelligence to Industry 4.0, and evaluates the global economics of increased robot density in smart factories and the impact on the human workforce.

### Your submission must address

1. **Collaborative robots**
- Assess the advantages and scope of collaborative robots over traditional industrial robot methods.
- Explain where cobots would or would not be suitable for the ASI application.
- Consider flexibility, payload, speed, guarding needs, and human-robot interaction.

2. **Advances in industrial robot technology**
- Investigate advances in industrial robot technology.
- Include relevant developments such as machine vision, AI, digital twins, Industrial Internet of Things, edge computing, simplified integration, cloud robotics, and virtual commissioning.
- Relate each advance to practical manufacturing benefit.

3. **Artificial intelligence and Industry 4.0**
- Analyse the benefits of artificial intelligence within industrial robotics and its contribution to Industry 4.0.
- Consider predictive maintenance, adaptive picking, quality inspection, scheduling optimisation, and data-driven performance improvement.

4. **Global economics and workforce impact**
- Evaluate the global economics of increased robot density in smart factories and the impact on the human workforce.
- Refer to major robot markets such as Japan, USA, China, South Korea, and Germany.
- Consider productivity, competitiveness, labour displacement, workforce retraining, new job roles, and long-term business resilience.
- Include an evidence-based judgement on the economic case for robotic automation in manufacturing.

- Analysis of AI contribution to Industry 4.0
