# Electronics Lab — General Safety Rules

**These rules apply to all persons working in the Electronics Lab. Read the equipment-specific operating procedures as well. If you are unsure, ask the supervisor before proceeding.**

---

## Entry and Authorisation

- Do not enter or begin work without authorisation from the supervisor or technician.
- Report any damaged equipment or unsafe condition before starting.
- Visitors and observers shall not touch any equipment.

## Personal Conduct

- Work deliberately. Rushed or distracted operation causes avoidable incidents.
- Do not work alone on live circuits.
- No mobile phones while operating live equipment.
- No food or drink in the lab.

## Electrical Safety

- Treat every circuit as live until confirmed isolated.
- Do not connect or disconnect leads with the supply energised unless the procedure permits.
- Verify voltage and current settings before energising any circuit.
- Never exceed the rated limits of any component, instrument, or wire.
- Verify isolation by measurement — do not assume the supply is off.
- Isolate power immediately if smoke, burning smell, sparks, or unexpected heat appear, then report it.

## Bench Power Supply

- Reset current limit to a low value before connecting any new circuit.
- Check polarity before connecting — reversed polarity destroys components.
- Start from the lowest voltage setting and increase slowly.
- Do not leave the current limit higher than the circuit requires.
- The CC indicator signals an unexpected fault — reduce or remove power and inspect.

## Oscilloscope

- Identify the circuit reference point before connecting the probe ground clip.
- The probe ground clip is not floating — connecting it incorrectly can short the circuit.
- Match probe attenuation to the channel sensitivity setting before measuring.
- A blank or missing trace does not mean the circuit is safe. Do not use the oscilloscope as a proof-of-dead instrument.
- Both channel ground clips share a common reference — check for conflicts on multi-channel setups.
- Do not exceed the probe or input voltage rating.

## Function Generator

- Confirm the safe input amplitude of the circuit before connecting.
- Begin at the lowest amplitude and increase slowly.
- The output is live whenever leads are connected — there is no load enable switch.
- Do not change waveform, range, or amplitude abruptly on a live circuit.
- Check for ground reference conflicts when using the generator with an oscilloscope simultaneously.

## Multimeter and Test Instruments

- Select the correct function and range before connecting leads.
- Never measure voltage using the current (A) input — this blows the internal fuse and can damage the meter.
- Inspect leads before use. Do not use leads with cracked insulation or loose tips.
- Do not use an instrument beyond its rated measurement category (CAT rating).

## Housekeeping and Bench Discipline

- Keep the bench clear — only items needed for the current task should be present.
- Route cables so they cannot create trip hazards or pull instruments off the bench.
- Do not leave energised circuits unattended. Isolate power before leaving the bench.
- Return all instruments to their correct positions after use.
- Remove faulty or suspect equipment from the bench immediately and report it.

## Component Handling

- Take care with component leads, solder joints, and PCB edges — they can cause cuts.
- Dispose of clipped leads and solder waste in the designated container, not on the bench or floor.
- Wash hands after handling solder or PCB materials, and before leaving the lab.

## Fault Response

- Stop work immediately if a component smokes, a lead becomes hot, or any abnormal behaviour appears.
- Isolate power first. Do not diagnose a live fault under pressure.
- Do not restart after an abnormal event until the cause has been identified and resolved.
- Report all faults, near misses, and damaged equipment to the supervisor before leaving.
- Do not return an instrument involved in an abnormal event to general use until it has been cleared.

---

*These rules do not replace equipment-specific safety operating procedures. Both apply at all times.*
