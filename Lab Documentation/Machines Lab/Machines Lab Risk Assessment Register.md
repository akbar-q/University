# Machines Lab Risk Assessment Register

This document provides a Machines Lab-specific risk assessment register using the format: **SL.NO**, **Equipment name**, **ID**, **Activity/Hazard Source**, **Potential Risks / Hazards**, **Risk Level**, **Control Measures / Mitigation**, and **Residual Risk**.

## 1. Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel

| SL.NO | Equipment name | ID | Activity/Hazard Source | Potential Risks / Hazards | Risk Level | Control Measures / Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel | 1 | High-voltage DC supply from non-isolated rectified mains | Electric shock, serious injury, fatal contact hazard | High | Treat all exposed terminals as live; isolate before wiring; use only approved leads; confirm zero energy before touching; staff supervision required | Low |
| 1 | Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel | 2 | Variable DC supply and three-phase AC output | Shock, arcing, short circuit, equipment damage | High | Verify wiring against circuit before energisation; use two-person check; never alter wiring live; keep leads routed and secured | Low |
| 1 | Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel | 3 | Unsleeved banana leads and exposed prongs | Accidental contact, bridging adjacent live points, short circuit | High | Remove damaged or poorly taped leads from service; keep unused leads clear; use shortest safe lead routing; inspect before use | Medium |
| 1 | Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel | 4 | Fuse rupture under short-circuit fault | Loud bang, arc event, flying debris, eye injury | High | Eye protection mandatory; do not stand in line with fault path; verify wiring before power-on; replace only with correct fuse type and rating | Low |
| 1 | Motor-Generator Trainer and Control Panel | 5 | Exposed rotating shaft, coupler, and belt drive | Entanglement, impact injury, trapping of hair or clothing | High | Tie back hair; remove jewellery; no reaching over rotating parts; maintain safe distance while running; stop machine before approach | Low |

## 2. Manual Ranging Multimeter

| SL.NO | Equipment name | ID | Activity/Hazard Source | Potential Risks / Hazards | Risk Level | Control Measures / Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 1 | Leads left in current socket during voltage measurement | Direct short circuit, arc, blown fuse, instrument damage | High | Always return red lead to normal voltage terminal after current measurement; verify socket before contact | Low |
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 2 | Measuring current in parallel instead of series | Short circuit, fuse rupture, circuit damage | High | Confirm aloud whether meter is in series or parallel use; use correct current socket and range | Low |
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 3 | Resistance or capacitance test on live circuit | Meter damage, false readings, exposure to live voltage | High | Isolate supply first; discharge stored energy; verify safe condition before resistance or capacitance measurement | Low |
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 4 | Manual range set too low | Overload, blown fuse, misleading result | Medium | Start on safe high range; step down carefully; confirm AC or DC mode before measurement | Low |
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 5 | Damaged leads or exposed probe tips | Shock, accidental bridging of terminals | Medium | Inspect leads before use; hold probes behind finger guards; remove damaged probes from service | Low |
| 2 | Manual Ranging Multimeter | 6 | Measuring near rotating machinery | Slip, entanglement, contact with live points | Medium | Keep leads away from moving parts; do not lean into machines; stop if safe body position cannot be maintained | Low |
