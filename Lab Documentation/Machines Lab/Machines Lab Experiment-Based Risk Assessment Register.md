# Machines Lab Experiment-Based Risk Assessment Register

This document provides an experiment-based Machines Lab risk assessment register using the format: **SL.NO**, **Experiment / Practical Title**, **ID**, **Activity/Hazard Source**, **Potential Risks / Hazards**, **Risk Level**, **Control Measures / Mitigation**, and **Residual Risk**.

## 1. DC Motor Experiments

| SL.NO | Experiment / Practical Title | ID | Activity/Hazard Source | Potential Risks / Hazards | Risk Level | Control Measures / Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Series DC Motor | 1 | Series motor started or allowed to run with inadequate mechanical load | Dangerous overspeed, thrown parts, severe rotating-machine hazard | High | Never run the series motor without the specified load arrangement; confirm load path before startup and keep all persons clear of rotating parts | Medium |
| 1 | Series DC Motor | 2 | Wrong armature or series-field wiring on non-isolated DC supply | Short circuit, fuse rupture, arc event, equipment damage, shock risk | High | Complete wiring only when isolated; use two-person circuit check before energisation; verify field and armature paths exactly against the experiment diagram | Low |
| 2 | Shunt DC Motor | 3 | Loss of shunt field, weak field, or incorrect field connection | Overspeed, unstable operation, high armature current, machine damage | High | Confirm field circuit continuity before startup; energise in the correct sequence required by the procedure; stop immediately on abnormal speed rise | Low |
| 2 | Shunt DC Motor | 4 | Manual measurements or adjustments made near rotating shaft and live terminals | Entanglement, probe slip, accidental short circuit, electric shock | High | Keep leads away from rotating parts; assign separate roles for control, load, observation, and recording; do not lean into the machine for readings | Low |
| 3 | Compound DC Motor | 5 | Series and shunt fields connected with wrong polarity or wrong compounding arrangement | Excess current, unstable torque, unexpected speed behaviour, heating | High | Check cumulative or differential field connections carefully before startup; verify against the intended circuit and do not energise uncertain wiring | Low |
| 3 | Compound DC Motor | 6 | Rapid load change or overloading during characteristic test | Belt instability, overheating, abrupt speed change, inaccurate results | Medium | Apply load gradually; use the designated load operator only; call readings aloud so the load operator keeps a safe body position | Low |

## 2. AC Machine Experiments

| SL.NO | Experiment / Practical Title | ID | Activity/Hazard Source | Potential Risks / Hazards | Risk Level | Control Measures / Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AC Generator | 1 | Generated voltage present at machine terminals while rotating | Electric shock, arcing, equipment damage, unsafe backfeed condition | High | Treat generator terminals as live during rotation; verify connections before drive-up; isolate before rewiring or touching terminals | Low |
| 1 | AC Generator | 2 | Incorrect excitation, wiring, or load connection | Overvoltage, overheating, unstable output, fuse rupture | High | Confirm the exact excitation and output circuit before startup; increase operating point gradually and stop on abnormal voltage or current | Low |
| 2 | AC Motor | 3 | Incorrect three-phase connection or wrong starter operation | Sudden reverse rotation, high inrush, mechanical shock, equipment damage | High | The lead operator only shall energise the starter; confirm phase connections and machine mode before power-on; keep all persons clear of the shaft line | Low |
| 2 | AC Motor | 4 | Load increased too quickly or machine allowed to stall | Belt heating, excessive current, loud fault event, unstable operation | High | Apply load in small controlled steps; monitor current continuously; reduce load and isolate if the machine labours or protection operates | Low |
| 3 | AC Motor and Generator Measurements | 5 | Multimeter or meter leads used in wrong mode on live AC circuits | Short circuit, blown fuse, arc, misleading measurements | High | State the intended measurement aloud before contact; verify lead sockets and AC mode; return red lead to the normal voltage socket after current tests | Low |

## 3. Solar Panel I-V Testing

| SL.NO | Experiment / Practical Title | ID | Activity/Hazard Source | Potential Risks / Hazards | Risk Level | Control Measures / Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solar Panel I-V Testing | 1 | Illuminated panel treated as de-energised when exposed to strong light | Live DC present at terminals, shock risk, accidental short circuit | Medium | Treat the panel as live whenever illuminated; cover or isolate it before wiring changes where the procedure allows; verify polarity before connection | Low |
| 1 | Solar Panel I-V Testing | 2 | Multimeter left in current mode or connected incorrectly during open-circuit/short-circuit measurements | Meter fuse rupture, short circuit, damaged panel leads, arc at terminals | High | Verify socket position and measurement method before every reading; confirm aloud whether measuring voltage or current; use the correct series or parallel arrangement | Low |
| 1 | Solar Panel I-V Testing | 3 | Load resistance or rheostat adjusted beyond rating during I-V sweep | Hot resistor surfaces, overheated load bank, damaged test equipment, fire risk | Medium | Use only the approved variable load or resistor arrangement; confirm power rating before test; allow components to cool before handling | Low |
| 1 | Solar Panel I-V Testing | 4 | Poor cable management or exposed clips during live testing | Accidental shorting, reversed polarity, misleading I-V data | Medium | Route leads neatly, secure clips, and keep unused leads clear of adjacent terminals; check polarity before each sweep | Low |
| 2 | Solar Panel I-V Testing Under Outdoor or High-Light Conditions | 5 | Bright light, hot panel surface, or unstable test setup | Heat discomfort, dropped panel, broken glass or frame damage, unreliable readings | Medium | Secure the panel before testing; avoid unstable hand-held measurements; allow for surface heating and use a clear measurement setup | Low |
