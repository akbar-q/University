LO4 Presentation — Marking Guidelines (grading rubric and guidance)

Purpose
- This document guides assessors in marking the LO4 presentation deliverable. It defines the assessment factors, evidence expectations, and how to convert observed quality into a grade band (Pass / Merit / Distinction). It is intentionally separate from the slide template so students do not include assessment labels in their slides.

How to use this guideline
- Review the student's completed presentation and supporting artefacts (report, code, test logs, appendices).
- Score each factor below on its evidence and quality (0–4 scale: 0=no evidence, 1=poor, 2=satisfactory, 3=good, 4=excellent).
- Sum the factor scores and map to grade bands using the guidance at the end.

Assessment factors (brief)
1. Coverage of current uses (evidence & accuracy)
   - Does the presentation identify key applications in the chosen sector?
   - Are claims supported by reputable references (datasheets, white papers, industry reports)?
2. Quality of case studies and examples
   - Are case studies concrete (vendor, specs, outcomes)?
   - Does the student critically note limitations and failure modes?
3. Trend analysis (breadth & depth)
   - Does the presentation explain relevant trends and why they matter for the sector?
   - Are trade-offs and technical constraints discussed (connectivity, power, latency)?
4. Technical understanding
   - Are architectures, protocols and key technology choices explained correctly?
   - Is there evidence of understanding of MCU choices, edge/cloud trade-offs, and constraints?
5. Research appraisal and critical thinking
   - Does the student summarise recent research and identify gaps or uncertainties?
   - Are ethical, social and economic implications considered and balanced?
6. Risk assessment and mitigation
   - Are risks identified (security, reliability, supply chain) and are mitigations proposed?
7. Evidence & references quality
   - Harvard-style references present; claims linked to sources; appendices contain supporting artefacts.
8. Presentation quality & communication
   - Slide clarity, logical flow, conciseness, speaker notes, and referencing of appendices.
9. Practicality & recommendations
   - Are recommendations actionable (pilot steps, KPIs, acceptance tests)?
   - Is there a clear roadmap for short/medium/long term?

Scoring and mapping to grade bands
- Scoring: each factor scored 0–4; total maximum = 36.

Suggested band thresholds (adjust to your assessment policy):
- Pass: total 18–24 with all critical factors (Coverage, Evidence, Presentation Quality) at least 2 or higher.
- Merit: total 25–30 with strong technical understanding and trend analysis (factors 3 & 4 >= 3).
- Distinction: total 31–36 with excellent research appraisal, balanced societal analysis, and practical, evidence-backed recommendations (factors 5,6,7,9 >= 3).

Notes and moderation
- Use moderation to resolve edge cases; where evidence is incomplete but other factors are strong, consider targeted feedback and a conditional re-submission.
- Document specific page/slide references when downgrading for missing evidence (e.g., "Slide 6: case study lacks vendor datasheet — score factor 2 as 1/4").

Feedback template (one-line per factor)
- Coverage of current uses: [score] — notes...
- Quality of case studies: [score] — notes...
- Trend analysis: [score] — notes...
- Technical understanding: [score] — notes...
- Research appraisal: [score] — notes...
- Risk assessment: [score] — notes...
- Evidence & references: [score] — notes...
- Presentation & communication: [score] — notes...
- Practicality & recommendations: [score] — notes...

Final grade and rationale
- Total score: X/36
- Recommended grade band: Pass / Merit / Distinction
- Rationale: one paragraph summarising the strongest evidence and key gaps that determined the grade.

Record-keeping
- Save the completed marking sheet alongside the student's submission (PDF) and record the total score in the cohort spreadsheet with assessor initials and date.
